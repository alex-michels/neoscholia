# CCAG: the overall landscape (checked 2026-10-26)

*Catalogus Codicum Astrologorum Graecorum*, Brussels 1898–1953: 12 tomes in 20 parts. Every title page and year below was checked against the OCR of the scans; vol. XI.1 was also checked against the page image.

## 0. Key findings

- **Scans:** all 20 parts are on archive.org. The best single item is the misnamed `ccag-8.4`, which holds every part.
- **Public domain in the EU and Russia:** I–VIII.4 and XI.1–2. All their editors died by 1950.
- **Protected (EU):** V.4 and IX.1–2 (Weinstock, d. 1971; Boer, d. 1980), and X (Delatte, d. 1964, protected until the end of 2034).
- **Vol. XII (Šangin):** public domain in the EU, but protected in Russia until 2059–2063. Šangin was repressed and rehabilitated in 1989.
- **Horoscopic astrology is already well covered in English:** Project Hindsight, Holden/AFA, Gramaglia–Dykes, and above all Levente László's HOROI Project, which aims to translate *all* Greek horoscopic texts.
- **Russian translations of horoscopic texts exist, mostly second-hand from the English.** There is one academic Russian translation of two CCAG horoscopes (Brol'–Kuzenkov 2013).
- **Machine English versions exist and must be acknowledged before any "first translation" claim:**
  - archive.org, all 20 parts (2026);
  - Source Library, vols. I and VII;
  - Scriptorai, vol. I.
- **Least-covered material, and the best niche for Neoscholia:** divination, magic and natural-omen texts, plus late Byzantine and post-Byzantine "popular" texts. Examples: brontologia, seismologia, selenodromia, kalandologia, plant lore, Melampus's lunar tracts, the vernacular texts in X and XII, and Cumont's *testimonia* on astrologers.

### Šangin's fate

- Arrested 14.11.1930 in the "Academic Case" (Академическое дело).
- Sentenced 10.2.1931 to 10 years of camp; sent to the Solovki camp administration at Kem'.
- Released 19.3.1932.
- Died in 1942 in evacuation in Makhachkala, of illness.
- **Rehabilitated in 1989.**

### Status as of 2026

**EU (life + 70; country of origin Belgium).**
- Public domain: I–VIII.4, XI.1–2 and XII (Šangin's term ended at the end of 2012).
- Protected:
  - X until 31.12.2034 (Delatte);
  - V.4, IX.1, IX.2 until 31.12.2041 (Weinstock);
  - the Porphyry edition in V.4 until 31.12.2050 (Boer).
- Any sui generis right in "critical and scientific publications" is capped at 30 years from publication (Term Directive 2006/116/EC, art. 5), so it has expired for every volume.
- What remains protectable in V.4, IX and X is the editors' own text: the Latin descriptions, prefaces, apparatus and conjectures. Whether a bare transcription of a Greek text is protectable is doubtful, but the conservative course is to avoid reproducing those volumes.

**Russia.**
- Civil Code art. 1281: life + 70.
  - §4: for a repressed author rehabilitated after death, the 70 years run from 1 January of the year after rehabilitation.
  - §5: +4 years if the author worked during the Great Patriotic War, or took part in it.
- Art. 1256 §4: a foreign work is protected only while it is not public domain in its country of origin, and never longer than there.
- Result for the foreign editors: same as the EU.
- Result for Šangin (a Soviet citizen, so Russian law applies in full): protected until 31.12.2059, or 31.12.2063 if the +4 years are applied on top. Vol. XII is therefore **not** safe for a Russian-facing site. At most, use only the bare Greek text, and treat even that as a risk.

This is orientation, not legal advice.

## 3. Translations that drew on CCAG

### a) Translation programmes

**Project Hindsight (R. Schmidt; Golden Hind Press), Greek Track 1993–98, plus *Definitions and Foundations* (2009).** Volumes that draw on CCAG:
- IIa, Anonymous of 379, *On the Fixed Stars* (CCAG V.1, ed. Cumont);
- IIb, Antiochus, *Thesaurus* fragments (CCAG I 143–164 and VII 107ff., ed. Boll; VIII.3 Antiochus summary);
- IX, *Teachings on Transits* (Dorotheus, Orpheus, Anubio, Valens);
- X, *The Astrological Record of the Early Sages in Greek*. By Hindsight's own description this is "all the material found in the CCAG" from astrologers before Dorotheus, Valens and Ptolemy: Hermes, Nechepso and Petosiris, Critodemus, "Zoroaster", "Pythagoras", Timaeus, Thrasyllus, Balbillus;
- *Definitions and Foundations* (2009): Antiochus with Porphyry, Rhetorius, Serapio, Thrasyllus, Antigonus.

The other volumes (Paulus, Ptolemy, Valens, Hephaestio I–II) use non-CCAG editions. Source: projecthindsight.net summaries and timeline PDF.

**J. H. Holden, American Federation of Astrologers (AFA):**
- *Porphyry the Philosopher: Introduction to the Tetrabiblos* and *Serapio of Alexandria: Astrological Definitions*, 2009 (some listings say 2010). Sources: CCAG V.4 (Boer–Weinstock) and the Serapio text catalogued in VIII.4.
- *Rhetorius the Egyptian: Astrological Compendium*, 2009. Rhetorius's chapters are scattered through CCAG I, VII, VIII.1 and VIII.4. Holden's base text was not verified from the book itself.

**E. J. Gramaglia (with B. Dykes, Cazimi Press):**
- *Astrological Works of Theophilus of Edessa*, 2017. Theophilus's Greek is edited mainly in CCAG (I, V.1, VIII.1, XI.1).
- Hephaistio, *Apotelesmatics* III, 2013 (from Pingree's Teubner edition).
- Serapio, *Definitions*, 2013, published on the Hellenistic Astrology website.
- Spanish-language work on Theophilus (academia.edu).

**B. Dykes:** his own translations are mainly from Arabic and Latin. Greek reaches him through Gramaglia, as above.

**D. G. Greenbaum:**
- *Late Classical Astrology: Paulus and Olympiodorus* (ARHAT 2001), not CCAG.
- *The Daimon in Hellenistic Astrology* (Brill 2016) translates passages and cites CCAG I, V.4, VII 107–122 and VIII.3. Which passages are translated was not verified.

**HOROI Project (Levente László, PhD candidate):** English translations on Patreon since May 2020. The full Patreon list was harvested: 450 posts. The public site horoiproject.com carries only two Rhetorius posts. The aim is all Greek horoscopic texts, and many items are CCAG texts:
- Rhetorius, *Explanation and Interpretation of All Astrology* (60+ posts, 2020–26);
- Teucer of Babylon (co-rising constellations; decans, with Rhetorius appendices);
- Julian of Laodicea (inceptions, military, medical, planets, travellers);
- Serapio; "Porphyry" excerpts from Antiochus; Porphyry's *Introduction*; the Antiochus summary;
- Anonymous of 379 (bright stars, passionate degrees, eyesight, Moon);
- Theophilus of Edessa (ingresses; "Second Edition", in progress in 2025–26);
- "Balchus" = Palchus, *Astrological Book* (21 parts, 2023–24);
- fragments of Nechepso–Petosiris, Thrasyllus, Balbillus, Critodemus, Timaeus, Antigonus, Heliodorus and Dorotheus;
- Stephanus of Alexandria, *Astrological Matters* (the Horoscope of Islam);
- Byzantine horoscopes: Constantine VII; Constantinople; cases dated 972, 984, 1001–1011 and 1006; further 10th–11th-century horoscopes;
- the anonymous commentary on Ptolemy; Maximus's paraphrase; Hippolytus/"Pythagoras"; "the Egyptians on comets"; the named degrees; the 28 lunar mansions; Greek Shādhān/Abū Maʿshar.

These titles have not yet been mapped one by one to CCAG volumes and pages. **Always check HOROI before choosing a horoscopic text.**

### b) Machine (AI) translations of CCAG

- **archive.org `vol.-8-part-3-1912-codicum-parisinorum-dual-language`** (uploader "astroji", 11.6.2026, Public Domain Mark): all 20 parts, "bilingual with English", claiming "all have been translated for 1st time". A sample of vol. I shows English interleaved with the OCR, covering both the catalogue entries and appendix texts (a Rhetorius chapter). It is unreviewed.
- **Source Library** (sourcelibrary.org; Embassy of the Free Mind, Amsterdam; AI translation with Gemini-based OCR; CC BY-SA). Status from its API on 2026-10-23:
  - vol. I: 185 of 204 pages translated;
  - vol. VII: 235 of 282 pages translated;
  - VIII.2 and III: imported, not yet processed.
- **Scriptorai**, GitHub `sadalsvvd/scriptorai-ccag-01`: an "AI-produced translation" of vol. I, CC0, May 2025. The repository `-ccag-02` is empty.

### c) Academic and other translations (examples found; not exhaustive)

- **Brol', R. V. & Kuzenkov, P. V.**, «Астрология и политика в Византии V в.», *Византийский временник* 72 (97), 2013, 166–198. This is the **first Russian translation** of two Palchus-group horoscopes: Leontius's coronation (484) and Pamprepius's nativity (440). The translation base is CCAG I and VIII.4, per secondary snippets; exact pages not verified.
- **Hygromanteia of Solomon** (ed. Heeg, CCAG VIII.2, pp. 143–165):
  - Torijano, *Solomon the Esoteric King* (Brill 2002), appendix 1, English;
  - Torijano in *Old Testament Pseudepigrapha: More Noncanonical Scriptures* (Eerdmans 2013), English;
  - Torijano 1999, Spanish;
  - Marathakis, *The Magical Treatise of Solomon* (2011), English.
  - No Russian translation was found.
  - Note: Heeg's introduction cites Ps.-Callisthenes I.1 (Nectanebo), a link to the Alexander Romance.
- **E. A. Fisher**, *GRBS* 59 (2019) 714–736: Greek preface to a brontologion in Petrop. Bibl. Publ. gr. 575, with English translation.
  - That manuscript is CCAG XII cod. 4. Its appendix excerpts include an astragalomantic oracle (p. 145) and an Apocalypse of Daniel.
  - Excerpts from other codices in the same appendix include *Preces magicae christianae* and *De septem herbis planetarum*.
- **Other examples:**
  - Neugebauer & Van Hoesen, *Greek Horoscopes* (1959): abridged English of many CCAG horoscopes.
  - Heilen, *Hadriani genitura* (2015): German, Antigonus.
  - Stegemann (1939/1943): German translation of Dorotheus's Greek fragments (per Wikipedia).
  - L. László, "Rhetorius, Zeno's Astrologer…", *DOP* 74 (2020) 329–350.
  - Festugière, *La Révélation d'Hermès Trismégiste* I: French versions of Hermetic astrological and botanical texts. Item-by-item coverage not verified.

### d) Russian translations

**Academic:** only Brol'–Kuzenkov 2013 was found. Searches for «Каталог греческих астрологических рукописей», ВДИ, ΣΧΟΛΗ, «Схолион», ВВ, «гигромантия», брон­тологий and similar terms gave nothing else. A Russian edition of Magdalino's *L'orthodoxie des astrologues* exists («Византийская астрология…»; bibliographic details not verified).

**Astrological community, mostly translated from English (per astro-profi.com):**
- **G. Zennikov ("G.Z."; spelling varies), publisher «Александр и Константин», 2016–2020:** Hindsight vols. III, IX, X and Companion; Hephaestion I; Antiochus's *Thesaurus* (via Rhetorius); Serapio.
- **G. Mirskoy (Boosty; from HOROI's English):** Porphyry/Antiochus, Anonymous of 379, Julian of Laodicea, Rhetorius chapters, the Zeno inceptions (Palchus), Constantine VII's nativity, 11th-century Byzantine charts.
- **Others:** L. Tvardovskaya (Porphyry, from Holden).

**Consequence:** for horoscopic texts, criterion (1) "no Russian translation" will often fail, at least for a second-hand version. Nothing was found for the divination and magic texts.

## 4. Digital resources

- **TLG:** coverage of CCAG texts could not be verified. The canon needs a subscription, and its search pages refuse scripted access.
  - LSJ's author list (on the TLG site) cites *Cat. Cod. Astr.* as the edition for Antiochus (I 108; VII 107), Julian of Laodicea, Harpocration and a Dorotheus supplement.
  - *Action:* check the TLG Canon author by author: Rhetorius, Theophilus, Palchus, Julian, Teucer, anonymous texts.
  - The free LBG (stephanus.tlg.uci.edu/lbg) is useful for Byzantine vocabulary. Whether it covers CCAG was not verified.
- **HathiTrust full-text search** works inside the volumes that are search-only (IX, XI, XII).
- **The OCR of the archive.org `ccag-8.4` item is Greek-aware.** Greek script is recognized in samples from I, V.4, VIII.1–4 and IX. In XI.2 and XII the Greek comes out as garbled Cyrillic, so read the page images there (`…/download/ccag-8.4/CCAG<vol>_jp2.zip/CCAG<vol>_jp2%2FCCAG<vol>_<nnnn>.jp2&ext=jpg`).
- **Pinakes** (pinakes.irht.cnrs.fr): the standard database of Greek manuscripts and texts. The connection failed from here on 2026-10-26.
- **eCAB** (UCLouvain/GREgORI): digital *Corpus des astronomes byzantins*, vols. 1–10. Free PDFs, a lemmatized and tagged corpus, and a cumulative index of texts and translations. It covers astronomy, not astrology, and its editions are modern.
- **PAL** (*Ptolemaeus Arabus et Latinus*, ptolemaeus.badw.de) has entries for Greek Ptolemaica, e.g. Pseudo-Porphyry (work/289).
- **No digital author-by-text index of the texts edited in CCAG was found.** The only route is the printed indices of each volume, or the OCR.
- **Useful bibliographies:** the BnF catalogue SRU, which gives per-volume records and Gallica links; and the CCAG section of de.wikisource.

## 5. Implications for choosing candidates

1. Prefer volumes I–VIII.4 and XI. Use X only as a Greek text without the editorial material, or wait until 2035. Avoid XII for a Russian-facing release.
2. Before choosing any horoscopic text, check HOROI, Hindsight, Holden and the Russian second-hand translations. The clearest gaps are the divination and magic texts. Examples:
   - Melampus's selenodromion, framed as written for "king Nechepso" and as a book found at Heliopolis under Psammetichus (VIII.4, appendix, cod. 68);
   - the brontologia and plant lore of VIII.3;
   - Cumont's *testimonia* on astrologers in VIII.3 and VIII.4;
   - the vernacular and late texts in X and XII;
   - the Spanish codices in XI.
3. Word the claim as "first Russian translation", or "first reviewed scholarly translation". Mention the existing machine English versions where they cover the text.
