**Open point (reviewer's «расширение» / 'widening')**

  - LSJ I.1 "dilatation" (Arist. PA 664a33: "ἔχειν δ. to have the power of dilatation");

  - Bailly "distension, dilatation … avec idée d'écartement".
- So there is real support for «расширение отверстий» / 'the widening of the holes'. "The extent [of the pipe] up to the holes" has no lexical support beyond the general "extension".

  - The edition's two references come from LSJ, and both passages do contain the word.
- Theon 64.2–6 (Adrastus): "Ἀριστόξενος μὲν γὰρ ἐπὶ τὸ δὶς διὰ πασῶν καὶ διὰ τεσσάρων τὸ τοῦ καθ' αὑτὸν πολυτρόπου διαγράμματος πεποίηται μέγεθος, οἱ δὲ νεώτεροι τὸ πεντεκαιδεκάχορδον τρόπον μέγιστον ἐπὶ τὸ τρὶς διὰ πασῶν καὶ τόνον διεστηκός".
  - The word names the moderns' fifteen-note scheme.
  - As Hiller prints the passage, the span given is three octaves and a tone, i.e. the compass of the whole set of keys, not the double octave.
  - Hiller's apparatus records that Bullialdus wrote "ἐπὶ τὸ δὶς διὰ πασῶν διεστηκός", and that A has a numeral (ιε΄) at this point.
  - So the composer is right that Theon does not give the double octave here. The passage still concerns a fifteen-note system.
- Ptolemy, Harm. 3.1: I could not read Düring's text. The only online copy (archive.org "die-harmonielehre-des-klaudios-ptolemaios") is a Z-Library upload, so I did not use it.
  - Wallis's Greek title, as given on sourcelibrary.org (https://sourcelibrary.org/book/harmonica-ptolemy/page-number/120), is "Πῶς διὰ τῆς τοῦ κανόνος ἐκθέσεως … τοῦ πεντεκαιδεκαχόρδου κανόνος". The chapter follows directly on the lyre-and-kithara chapter (2.16), which agrees with LSJ's "3.1 tit."
  - The chapter's opening, in the OCR there, turns to completing the canon to the double octave, and p. 121 has "δεκαπέντε … δὶς διὰ πασῶν μεγέθους" (raw OCR).
  - The book's 2.4 title is "Περὶ συστήματος τελείου, καὶ ὅτι μόνον τοιοῦτο τὸ δὶς διὰ πασῶν".
  - A secondary source (https://polskabibliotekamuzyczna.pl/encyklopedia/ptolemeusz/?lang=en) says Ptolemy used a canon "with one string for each of the 15 notes of the double octave".
  - So in Ptolemy the word is used of a fifteen-string instrument built for the two-octave system. The composer's "neither passage concerns the two-octave system" does not hold for Ptolemy (moderate confidence: Düring not seen).
- The edition's "the usual term for a fifteen-note system" overstates. There are three attestations, and LSJ's sense is "with fifteen strings". Reported as a question.
