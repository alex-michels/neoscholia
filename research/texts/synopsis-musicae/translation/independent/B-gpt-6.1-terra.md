## Summary

The text combines mathematical ratio theory with a theory of melodic genera. The most difficult passages connect indivisible arithmetic units to continuous lengths and uncomposed melodic intervals. The printed numbers eight and a half and eight and a quarter do not state their denominator, so the ratios 17:16 and 33:32 are only possible interpretations. The tone 9:8 cannot be halved as a pitch interval by simply halving its numerical difference. The assertion that a fourth contains two tones, a semitone, and a further increment conflicts with a fourth of 4:3 if the 9:8 tone and an exact half-tone are used. The interval names with twice the fourth and twice the fifth do not match the magnitudes and ratios supplied in the same account. The overlapping two octaves explain fifteen distinct notes in the stated sixteen-string count. The Paris-only passages preserve additional arguments and are marked only at their actual opening and closing boundaries. The corrupt word in U050 has not been silently corrected, and the syntax of U058 remains ambiguous. The terminology is consistent where possible, though several of the author’s note names and interval names are unusual. The attribution of the diatonic genus to Plato and the physical account of weights and wind instruments need specialist review. The reviewer should first examine U018, U035–U041, U050 and U058 against the Greek and the supplied apparatus. External checking was selective; many parallels and lexical identifications remain marked as from memory rather than presented as verified.
