*Background note for the translators of Neoscholia, compiled 27 October 2026. Marks: **[✔]** checked in the source named; **[~]** taken from a secondary source and not checked in the original; **[?]** could not be verified. Do not cite [~] or [?] items as facts without checking them.*

## 1. The work

**What it is.** The work is an anonymous Byzantine school compendium. It opens with a synopsis of logic (Aristotle's *Organon*), which is followed by a synopsis of the four mathematical sciences: arithmetic, music, geometry and astronomy. The music section is the second of the four. In the Heidelberg manuscript of 1040 (see below) the whole book is divided into five numbered parts, Α–Ε: logic, arithmetic, music, geometry, astronomy (Rose 1867, 467) [✔]. The section titles are metrical paraphrases of the word σύνοψις (Rose 1867, 467) [✔]. For example, the arithmetic is headed "Ἐνταῦθ' ἀριθμῶν συντομωτέρα φράσις".

- Title of the mathematical part in the editio princeps: *Σύνταγμα εὐσύνοπτον εἰς τὰς τέσσαρας μαθηματικὰς ἐπιστήμας, ἀριθμητικήν, μουσικήν, γεωμετρίαν καὶ ἀστρονομίαν*. Title of the music section as listed there: *Τῆς Μουσικῆς σύνοψις ἠκριβωμένη*. Both are from the BnF record of Venice 1532, which gives them in transliteration [✔ catalogue].
- In our base print (Basel), the heading on p. 23 is set in the order **ΤΗΣ ΜΟΥΣΙΚΗΣ ΗΚΡΙΒΩΜΕΝΗ ΣΥΝΟΨΙΣ** [✔ scan]. The 1532 order, *σύνοψις ἠκριβωμένη*, scans as a twelve-syllable verse; the Basel order does not. Note this; do not "correct" the Basel heading.

**Date.** The author gives "the present year" as **6516** of the Byzantine world era, i.e. AD 1007/1008.
- This happens twice, both times in the **astronomy** section, not in the music. In Heiberg's edition the places are **p. 108.12–14 and p. 109.9–10** (Zeegers-Vander Vorst 1963, 129 n. 2) [✔].
- Rose (1867, 467) quotes both places [✔]:
  - the first counts the four-year periods (τετραετηρίδες) "from the reign of Philip up to now, that is the year ͵ϛφιϛʹ";
  - the second says "αἱ δὲ κατὰ τὸ νῦν ͵ϛφιϛʹ ἔτος…".
- Rose notes that the figures accompanying the year do not agree with each other. He accepts the year all the same, because it is given the same way twice, and the Palatinus of 1040 has it twice as well (with an erasure at the first place).
- Benakis (2018, 101) dates the work "between 1008 and 1040" [✔]. The upper limit is the dated Palatine manuscript.
- C. M. Taisbak wrote a study of the date: "The Date of Anonymus Heiberg, Anonymi Logica et Quadrivium", *CIMAGL* 39 (1981) 97–102 [✔ bibliographic data]. We could not reach its content [?].
- The Basel print we use has only the music section in our scans, so the date does not appear in them.

**Attribution.**
- Arsenios Apostolis, the editor of the editio princeps (Venice 1532), put forward Psellus only as a guess, in his preface. Later printings dropped the preface and put Psellus on the title page (Rose 1867, 465) [✔].
- Michael Psellus was born in 1018. A work dated 1007/8 cannot be his (Zeegers-Vander Vorst 1963, 129) [✔].
- Manuscripts name other authors [✔ Zeegers, citing Heiberg pp. xii–xviii]:
  - Psellus;
  - Gregory of Nazianzus (Par. gr. 2062; Rose calls this title "pulled out of thin air");
  - Euthymios (Par. gr. 2494);
  - a monk Gregory (Vat. gr. 15, Vat. gr. 1026, Laur. 58.20).
- Laur. plut. 87.10 contains the whole book under a heading that names "the humble Gregory", who wrote it ἐν μονοτρόποις, "among the solitaries" (Rose 1867, 466–467) [✔].
- Rose and Krumbacher (*GBL*², 437) accepted "Gregorius monachus" as the author. Zeegers objects that hundreds of monks were called Gregory [✔].
- The logic was printed as the work of "Gregorius Aneponymus" by J. Wegelin (Augsburg 1600). The BnF record confirms the book [✔]. That this is the same logic is stated by Rose [✔]; we have not compared the texts.
- Heiberg's neutral title, *Anonymi Logica et Quadrivium*, gave the author his modern name, **"Anonymus Heiberg"**. Benakis (2018) still says "Monk Gregory Aneponymous". We call the author **the Anonymous** and the work **"the Quadrivium of 1008"**.

**Manuscripts.** We have only second-hand information.
- **Heidelberg, Pal. gr. 281**, dated 14 January 1040. According to Rose (1867, 467) [✔] it contains the whole book without the author's name: logic from f. 1, "τέλος τῆς φιλοσοφίας" on f. 50, arithmetic from f. 51, "τέλος τῆς ἀστρονομίας" on f. 114.
  - A digitisation exists at the Heidelberg University Library: `digi.ub.uni-heidelberg.de/diglit/cpgraec281` [✔ that it exists; we could not open it because of a bot check].
  - We have not located the folios of the music section [?].
  - This manuscript was written 32 years after the work. It is the best control we can reach for doubtful places in the print.
- Others named in the literature [✔ Zeegers/Rose]:
  - Par. suppl. gr. 677, Par. gr. 2136, Par. gr. 3067, Vat. gr. 1144: these give the work as Psellus';
  - Par. gr. 2062, Par. gr. 2494, Vat. gr. 15, Vat. gr. 1026;
  - Laur. 58.20 and Marc. gr. 529: the work is absorbed into Joseph Rhakendytes' 14th-century encyclopaedia;
  - Laur. 87.10;
  - Coisl. 384 and Vind. phil. gr. 174: logic and quadrivium still together.
- The manuscript tradition is large. We do not know Heiberg's sigla or stemma [?].

**Later use.** Di Mambro (2020) discusses three Byzantine quotations of the music section [✔ abstract only; article closed-access, content not seen]:
- the well-known one by Manuel Bryennius;
- one by John Zonaras;
- one by Michael Italicus.

Joseph Rhakendytes (14th c.) took the whole mathematical part into his encyclopaedia (Rose 1867, 466) [✔].

**Doctrine and sources (what scholars say).**
- Benakis (2018, 101–102) [✔]:
  - the treatment follows the Aristoxenian "harmonicists" up to a point;
  - its core, the doctrine of intervals, is Pythagorean, "as expounded by … Theon of Smyrna";
  - "at a critical point" the author switches from Aristoxenian summation of intervals to Pythagorean arithmetic of ratios, so the analysis runs on two levels at once;
  - Mizler (1746) picked out the prologue on cosmic harmony.
- Gertsman (1993, 66–67) [✔]: the music section "contains almost the whole set of the main paragraphs of ancient theory".
- Gertsman 2022, ch. 4, has nine sections [✔ table of contents only]:
  - "Following Pythagorean science";
  - "A short summary of the whole";
  - "A musico-mathematical excursus";
  - "On composite and incomposite intervals";
  - "A new classification of intervals";
  - "Return to ancient Pythagoreanism";
  - "The modal constructions of ancient music";
  - and two others.
- **Parallels are your job.** No study we could read gives passage-by-passage sources. Test each section against:
  - Nicomachus, *Enchiridion* (now also ed. S. Di Mambro, Pisa–Rome: Fabrizio Serra 2025, with Italian translation [~ BMCR 2026.04.39]);
  - Theon of Smyrna (Hiller);
  - Ptolemy, *Harmonics*;
  - Cleonides and Aristides Quintilianus;
  - the *Sectio canonis*.

## 3. The base edition used here: Basel [1556?] (Xylander)

- **Copy:** HathiTrust `ien.35552000251339`, Northwestern University copy, public domain. Greek music section on scans 189–199, **printed pp. 23–33**. Xylander's Latin version on scans 44–53, **pp. 40–49** of the separately paginated Latin part [✔].
- **Layout:** small octavo, about 20–22 lines a page.
  - Running heads: *Σύνταγμα.* on rectos, *Εἰς Μουσικήν* on versos.
  - Signatures and catchwords at the foot (e.g. β4 on p. 23, γ on p. 33).
  - p. 23 begins with the last lines of the arithmetic, closed by "Τῶν περὶ Ἀριθμητικὴν εἴληφε τέλος ἡ σύνοψις". Then the heading and a woodcut initial Μ: "Μουσικὴν οἱ παλαιοὶ συνέχειν εἶπον τὸ πᾶν…".
  - p. 33 ends with "Τέλος τῆς Μουσικῆς".
- **Xylander's marginal notes** are keyed to * and † in the text. They occur on several pages, at least pp. 25, 28, 29, 31 and 32. Some supply words, e.g. p. 25 "* διάτονος μέσων addo", "* τοῦ ἐννέα πρὸς addo", "† iterum eadem uerba addo". Others condemn words, e.g. p. 29 "* abundant omnino, & delenda sunt". **They are Xylander's conjectures, not the text.** The printed text is the base; the notes are an apparatus to report and weigh.
- **Figures:** p. 26 has marginal **dot figures**, rows of points, beside the argument about numbers and the division of the tone. Describe them in a note.
- **Note names in this print (p. 25):** they differ from the textbook forms. Watch especially for:
  - **διάτονος** used for the lichanos/paranete position (ὑπάτη διάτονος [sic], διάτονος διεζευγμένη, διάτονος ὑπερβολαίων);
  - **παράμεσος** (masculine);
  - nominative forms such as **τρίτη διεζευγμένη** instead of the genitive plural διεζευγμένων.

  The list has only fourteen names, because διάτονος μέσων is missing; Xylander supplies it in the margin, and Mizler (1746, 176–177 n. 4) already noted the gap. Translate what is printed and record the problem.
- **Proposed sigla for this package:**
  - **B** = Basel print (base);
  - **B^mg** = Xylander's margin;
  - **Xyl.** = Xylander's Latin;
  - **V** = Venice 1532 (not consulted);
  - **Miz.** = Mizler 1746;
  - **Heib.** = Heiberg 1929 (not available);
  - **P** = Pal. gr. 281.
