## Method

Their renderings were read in `composed-g1/g2/g4.json`.

- **Grammar.** Allen & Greenough, *New Latin Grammar* (1903), archive.org.
- **Theorists.**

  - For Hiller and Jan only the theorists' own text was read, not the apparatus or the editors' notes.
- **Other authors.**

## Changes to the notes (ru, en and source together)

### 1. V03, note 1 (anchor «вселенная держится музыкой» / "the universe is held together by music"): text and source
- **Was:** «(цицероновское слово: in universitate rerum; universitatem omnia continentem)» / "(a Ciceronian word: in universitate rerum; universitatem omnia continentem)".
- **Now:** «(цицероновское слово: in universitate rerum; universitatemque rerum, qua omnia continerentur)» / "(a Ciceronian word: in universitate rerum; universitatemque rerum, qua omnia continerentur)".
- **Reason.**
  - The phrase *universitatemque omnia continentem* is the Baiter–Kayser reading of N. D. 1.39, which L&S quotes.
  - The current text has instead *communemque rerum naturam universam atque omnia continentem*. A few lines later in the same section it has *universitatemque rerum, qua omnia continerentur* (The Latin Library). The new wording is in N. D. 1.39 whichever reading is followed.
- **Source corrected as well.**
  - The source said "universitas II (Cic. N. D. 1.39; 1.120)". In L&S both Cicero citations stand under sense I ("the whole"; 1.43.120 glossed "in the universe"). Sense II.A ("the universe") cites Cic. *Univ.*
  - L&S *contineo*: the note's main sense, 'held together', is I.A.1. The local sense is I.B.3.b, which was the only one cited.
  - Allen & Greenough § 431 was checked and § 403.b n. 2 added. Both say the ablative with *contineri* is probably locative, which is the basis of the note's second reading.

### 2. V16, note 1 (anchor «нестройный и неблагозвучный» / "out-of-tune and discordant"): text
- **Was:** «vox absona atque absurda (De or. 3.41)» / "vox absona atque absurda (De or. 3.41)".
- **Now:** «vox … absona atque absurda (De or. 3.41)» / "vox … absona atque absurda (De or. 3.41)".
- **Reason.**
  - Cicero's words are *mollis vox aut muliebris aut quasi extra modum absona atque absurda* (De or. 3.41, The Latin Library). The words quoted in the note are not continuous in the text.
  - L&S s.v. *absonus* prints the condensed form. The ellipsis makes the quotation exact.
- **Source.**
  - Cicero's full wording is now given.
  - L&S s.v. *absonus* was checked (it was "[checked: translators A, C]").
  - "Latin of X13" was added for Xylander's *dissonus* / *consonus*.

### 3. V17, note 2 (anchor «гипата диатоническая» / "hypate diatonos"): text and source
- **Was:**
  - «… как возникла форма Вине, неизвестно. (2) … (3) … (4) Вине пишет Paramese, Ксиландр — paramesos.»
  - "… how Vinet's form arose is not known. (2) … (3) … (4) Vinet writes Paramese, Xylander paramesos."
- **Now:**
  - «… как возникла эта форма, неизвестно. (2) … (3) … Отличия (1)–(3) есть и у Ксиландра (X14). (4) Вине пишет Paramese, Ксиландр — paramesos.»
  - "… how the form arose is not known. (2) … (3) … Xylander has (1)–(3) as well (X14). (4) Vinet writes Paramese, Xylander paramesos."
- **Reason.**
  - Xylander's list (X14) also has *hypate diatonos*, the movable notes as *diatonos meson / diezeugmene / hyperbolaeon*, and *trite / diatonos / nete diezeugmene* in the nominative singular. The departures (1)–(3) are therefore not Vinet's own, and "Vinet's form" misled.
  - Only (4) separates the two versions.
- **Claims verified.**
  - Boethius I.22, p. 214.7–8: *lichanos hypaton diatonos*.
  - Boethius I.20: *lichanos hypaton*.
  - Theon p. 88.17–18: ὑπερυπάτη … ἡ καὶ διάτονος ὑπατῶν, in the division of the canon that p. 87 ascribes to Thrasyllus.
- Source: page and line references added, plus "Latin of X14".

### 4. V32, note 3 (anchor «соединённых и слитых» / "joined and fused together"): text and source
- **Was:**
  - «… у Ксиландра — coalitus. По-русски оба переданы «слияние, слитый», по-английски у Вине fusion, fused, у Ксиландра coalescence.»
  - "… Xylander's coalitus. In Russian both are «слияние, слитый»; in English Vinet's word is 'fusion, fused', Xylander's 'coalescence'."
- **Now:**
  - «… у Ксиландра — coalitus (X51; в X27 — причастие coalescentibus). По-русски оба переданы словами «слияние», «слитый», «сливаться», по-английски у Вине fusion, fused, у Ксиландра coalescence, coalescing.»
  - "… Xylander's coalitus (X51; in X27 the participle coalescentibus). In Russian both are rendered with «слияние», «слитый», «сливаться»; in English Vinet's words are 'fusion, fused', Xylander's 'coalescence, coalescing'."
- **Reason.**
  - The source cited only "Latin of X27". X27 (the parallel to V32) has *ex coalescentibus in unum*, not *coalitus*. The noun *ex coalitu* is in X51, the parallel to V59.
  - The composed translations render X27 as «сливающихся» / "coalescing" and X51 as «слиянием» / "coalescence". The note now matches them.
- Source: "Latin of V57, V59, X27 and X51; composed ru/en of X27, X51, V57, V59 [checked]".

### 5. V33, note 1 (anchor «квартой, квинтой и октавой» / "the fourth, the fifth and the octave"): text and source
- **Was:** «Буквальный смысл он объясняет в V34.» / "He explains the literal sense in V34."
- **Now:** «Названия он объясняет в V34, где Dia-pason — «через восемь звуков или струн» (per octo sonos aut chordas).» / "He explains the names in V34, where Dia-pason is 'through eight notes or strings' (per octo sonos aut chordas)."
- **Reason.**
  - The note glosses Dia-pason as 'through all [the strings]' (LSJ s.v. διαπασῶν, checked) and then said that Vinet explains the literal sense in V34.
  - Vinet explains Dia-tessaron and Dia-pente literally, but Dia-pason as *per octo sonos aut chordas* (V34; again *per octo* in V35). He does not explain it as "through all"; Xylander does, in X29: *per omnes, hoc est, per octo*. The reader would have been misled.
- Source: "Latin of V34" added; LSJ checked.
