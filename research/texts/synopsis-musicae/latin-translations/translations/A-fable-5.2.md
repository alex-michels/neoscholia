# Two Renaissance Latin versions of the music section of the anonymous Byzantine *Quadrivium* (1008): an independent translation into Russian and English

- **Translator:** Claude Fable 5.2 (claude-fable-5-2), Anthropic
- **Date:** 2026-11-20
- **Web access:** yes (dictionaries, grammars and the theorists only)
- **Subagents:** a team of agents: a translator and a reviser for each version, and an assembler

Each segment gives the Latin reading text with its reference (page.line of the printing), the Russian translation, the English translation, and then the notes, checks and doubts. Words added for sense stand in [square brackets]; the numbers in square brackets after a ratio name are the ratio that the Latin names. A paragraph mark (¶) shows where a segment begins a paragraph of the printing. Every reference is marked [checked] (the source was opened and the place read) or [from memory].

## Xylander (Basel: Oporinus, [1556], pp. 40–49)

---

**X01** · Xyl. 40.1–2 · title

> **Latin.** Exacta summa hic, evidensque Musicae.

**Русский.** Здесь — точный и ясный свод музыки.

**English.** Here is an exact and clear summary of Music.

*Notes.*

- RU: Заглавие раздела у Ксиландра; summa — «свод, краткое изложение целого».  
  EN: Xylander's heading of the section; summa is a ‘sum, compendious account of the whole’.  
  Source: Lewis & Short, s.v. summa [from memory]

---

**X02** · Xyl. 40.3–7 · ¶ 1

> **Latin.** Universum hoc, antiqui Musica contineri perhibuerunt. Nihil enim in rerum natura absque commensuratione et proportione consistit:

**Русский.** Древние утверждали, что эта вселенная держится музыкой. Ведь ничто в природе вещей не существует без соразмерности и пропорции;

**English.** The ancients declared that this universe is held together by Music. For nothing in the nature of things subsists without commensuration and proportion;

*Notes.*

- RU: commensuratio et proportio — парное выражение, которое проходит через всё вступление; commensuratio — позднее слово (Боэций) со значением «соразмерность, симметрия». Proportio у Ксиландра далее (X25, X35–38) означает и числовое отношение интервала, как у Боэция (proportio в отличие от proportionalitas); перевод «пропорция» сохраняет его слово.  
  EN: commensuratio et proportio is a pair that runs through the whole proem; commensuratio is a late word (Boethius) meaning ‘symmetry, due measure’. Later (X25, X35–38) Xylander uses proportio for the numerical ratio of an interval, as Boethius does (proportio as against proportionalitas); the rendering ‘proportion’ keeps his word.  
  Source: Lewis & Short and Gaffiot, s.v. commensuratio (Boeth. Mus. 1, 31), DMLBS s.v. commensuratio, via Logeion [checked]; Boethius, De institutione arithmetica 2.40 on proportio / proportionalitas [from memory]

**X03** · Xyl. 40.7–12

> **Latin.** neque quicquam eorum quae fiunt, sine debita commensuratione et proportione si fiat, recte productum natumve esse dicitur: sive id ab arte efficiatur, sive a natura: et sive sub sensum illud cadat, sive solo intellectu perspiciatur.

**Русский.** и ни о чём из возникающего, если оно возникает без должной соразмерности и пропорции, не говорят, что оно произведено или рождено правильно, — создаётся ли оно искусством или природой, подпадает ли под чувство или усматривается одним лишь умом.

**English.** nor is anything among the things that come to be said to have been rightly produced or brought forth, if it comes to be without due commensuration and proportion: whether it is wrought by art or by nature, and whether it falls under sense or is discerned by the intellect alone.

**X04** · Xyl. 40.13–15 · ¶ 2

> **Latin.** Caeterum Musica ipsa per se commensuratio quaedam omnino et proportionalitas est, nimirum harmonia universi existens.

**Русский.** Музыка же сама по себе есть всецело некая соразмерность и пропорциональность, будучи, разумеется, гармонией вселенной.

**English.** Music itself, moreover, is in its own right altogether a certain commensuration and proportionality, being, to be sure, the harmony of the universe.

*Notes.*

- RU: Здесь вместо proportio стоит proportionalitas (у Боэция — «пропорция» как равенство отношений); различие слов сохранено: «пропорциональность».  
  EN: Here proportionalitas stands in place of proportio (in Boethius, ‘proportion’ as a likeness of ratios); the difference of words is kept: ‘proportionality’.  
  Source: Lewis & Short, Gaffiot, DMLBS, s.v. proportionalitas (Boeth. Arithm. 2, 40), via Logeion [checked]

**X05** · Xyl. 40.15–20

> **Latin.** Fortassis et ipsam divinam naturam haud inconvenienter sui ipsius et universi harmoniam quispiam dixerit: siquidem in Deo concinnatus, et ad consonantiam coaptatus mundus, bene, atque adeo quam optime se habere videtur.

**Русский.** Пожалуй, и саму божественную природу кто-нибудь вполне уместно назвал бы гармонией самой себя и вселенной: ведь мир, слаженный в Боге и прилаженный к консонансу, устроен, как видно, хорошо и даже как нельзя лучше.

**English.** Perhaps one might not unfittingly call even the divine nature itself the harmony of itself and of the universe: since the world, framed in God and fitted to consonance, is seen to be in a good, and indeed in the best possible, state.

*Notes.*

- RU: concinnatus, coaptatus: глаголы concinnare («ладно соединять, устраивать») и coaptare («прилаживать», слово церковной латыни) у Ксиландра постоянно передают «слаживание» гармонии (ср. X12, X44, X45, X47, X56); в переводе первый — «слаживать», второй — «прилаживать». Consonantia здесь — «согласное звучание» в широком смысле, но по принятому условию переведено «консонанс». Haud inconvenienter — литота («не неуместно»).  
  EN: concinnatus, coaptatus: the verbs concinnare (‘to join fitly, arrange’) and coaptare (‘to fit together’, a word of ecclesiastical Latin) are Xylander's standing words for the ‘fitting together’ of harmony (cf. X12, X44, X45, X47, X56); the first is rendered ‘to frame’, the second ‘to fit’. Consonantia is here ‘concord’ in a broad sense, but is rendered ‘consonance’ by the project's convention. Haud inconvenienter is a litotes (‘not unfittingly’).  
  Source: Lewis & Short, s.vv. concinno, coapto, via Logeion [checked]

**X06** · Xyl. 40.20–21

> **Latin.** Tanto nimirum Musicam illi dignati sunt miraculo.

**Русский.** Вот сколь великого удивления удостоили они музыку.

**English.** Of so great a wonder, evidently, did they deem Music worthy.

*Notes.*

- RU: illi — «древние» из X02. Miraculum — «диво, предмет удивления» (не «чудо» в церковном смысле): букв. «столь великого дива удостоили».  
  EN: illi are the ‘ancients’ of X02. Miraculum is ‘a wonder, a thing to marvel at’ (not a ‘miracle’ in the ecclesiastical sense): literally ‘they deemed Music worthy of so great a marvel’.  
  Source: Lewis & Short, s.v. miraculum (‘a wonderful, strange, or marvellous thing, a wonder, marvel’; ‘digna miraculo’, Plin. 12, 1, 5), via Logeion [checked]

*Doubts.*

- *Tanto ... dignati sunt miraculo*
  - Issue: miraculum: the wonder that Music is, or the admiration paid to it
  - Options: ‘deemed Music worthy of so great a wonder’ / ‘honoured Music with so great admiration’
  - Choice: ‘wonder’ in English; «удивление» in Russian (in its sense of ‘wonder’), where the literal «дива» is not idiomatic; the two now agree
  - Evidence: Lewis & Short, s.v. miraculum, via Logeion [checked]

**X07** · Xyl. 40.21–41.3

> **Latin.** Ea quando in omnibus conspicitur, atque universa temperat, exornatque, tum sensui voluptatem, congruentiam autem (harmoniam dico) Naturae, beatitatem denique intellectui largitur: nos de ea Musica quae in sensibus, aut potius uno sensuum auditu consideratur, paucis disseremus.

**Русский.** Поскольку она усматривается во всём, всё упорядочивает и украшает и к тому же дарует чувству наслаждение, природе — согласованность (я разумею гармонию), а уму, наконец, — блаженство, мы кратко скажем о той музыке, которая рассматривается в чувствах, или, вернее, в одном из чувств — слухе.

**English.** Since it is seen in all things, and tempers and adorns the whole, and moreover bestows pleasure on sense, congruence (I mean harmony) on Nature, and lastly blessedness on the intellect, we shall discourse briefly of that Music which is considered in the senses, or rather in one of the senses, hearing.

*Notes.*

- RU: Тройное деление: музыка чувственная, природная и умопостигаемая; трактат ограничивается первой. Вставка «harmoniam dico» — пояснение к слову congruentia внутри самого перевода Ксиландра. Ср. у Боэция деление на musica mundana, humana и instrumentalis (иное по составу).  
  EN: A threefold division: music of sense, of nature and of the intellect; the treatise confines itself to the first. The parenthesis harmoniam dico is a gloss on congruentia inside Xylander's own version. Compare Boethius' division into musica mundana, humana and instrumentalis (different in content).  
  Source: Boethius, De institutione musica, ed. Friedlein, read in the Latin Wikisource transcription of that edition, 1.2 (‘Tres esse musicas’: mundana, humana, and that which ‘in quibusdam constituta est instrumentis’) [checked]

*Doubts.*

- *Ea quando ... tum sensui voluptatem ... largitur: nos ...*
  - Issue: quando causal or temporal; tum correlative or simply ‘and moreover’
  - Options: ‘since it ... and bestows’ / ‘when it ..., then it bestows’
  - Choice: causal quando, with the whole period as the ground for nos ... disseremus; the colon before nos marks the main clause
  - Evidence: Allen & Greenough on causal quando [from memory]

**X08** · Xyl. 41.4–5 · ¶ 3

> **Latin.** Sonus est, vocis nullo intervallo divisae harmoniae particeps intensio, seu tenor.

**Русский.** Звук есть причастное гармонии напряжение, или удержание, голоса, не разделённого никаким интервалом.

**English.** A note is a tension, or sustained holding, of a voice divided by no interval, [a tension] that partakes of harmony.

*Notes.*

- RU: Определение из трёх признаков: (1) intensio seu tenor — «напряжение, или удержание» голоса на одной высоте (intensio у Цензорина — «высота тона»; tenor — «непрерывное удержание», у Квинтилиана «тон, ударение»); (2) nullo intervallo divisae — без протяжения в высоту, ср. X12, где звук сравнён с точкой; (3) harmoniae particeps — пригодность к мелодии, ср. X12–13. Слово intensio в определении звука — боэциевское: «Sonus igitur est vocis casus emmeles, id est aptus melo, in unam intensionem». Ср. также определения у Никомаха («не имеющее ширины напряжение мелодического голоса») и Клеонида («мелодическое попадание голоса на одну высоту»).  
  EN: A definition by three marks: (1) intensio seu tenor, the ‘tension, or holding’ of the voice at one pitch (intensio in Censorinus is ‘pitch’; tenor is ‘an uninterrupted holding’, in Quintilian ‘tone, accent’); (2) nullo intervallo divisae, without extension in pitch, cf. X12, where the note is compared to a point; (3) harmoniae particeps, fitness for melody, cf. X12–13. Intensio in the definition of the note is Boethius' word: ‘Sonus igitur est vocis casus emmeles, id est aptus melo, in unam intensionem’. Compare also the definitions in Nicomachus (‘a breadthless pitch of melodic voice’) and Cleonides (‘a melodic incidence of the voice upon one pitch’).  
  Source: Lewis & Short, s.vv. intensio (Censor. 12), tenor (Quint. 1, 5, 22–26), via Logeion [checked]; Boethius, De institutione musica, ed. Friedlein, read in the Latin Wikisource transcription of that edition, 1.8 [checked]; Nicomachus, Enchiridion 12, and Cleonides, Isagoge 1, in Jan, Musici scriptores Graeci [from memory]

*Doubts.*

- *vocis nullo intervallo divisae harmoniae particeps intensio*
  - Issue: divisae may go with vocis or with harmoniae
  - Options: ‘a tension of the voice undivided by any interval, partaking of harmony’ / ‘a tension of the voice partaking of a harmony divided by no interval’
  - Choice: with vocis: particeps is nominative and can only go with intensio, so that harmoniae depends on particeps; divisae (genitive) is then most naturally taken with vocis, and X12 explains the two marks separately (the note is indivisible; it partakes of harmony)
  - Evidence: X12 of this text; Nicomachus, Enchiridion 12 [from memory]
  - Alternative (RU): Звук есть напряжение, или удержание, голоса, причастное гармонии, не разделённой никаким интервалом.
  - Alternative (EN): A note is a tension, or sustained holding, of the voice that partakes of a harmony divided by no interval.

**X09** · Xyl. 41.5–7

> **Latin.** Intervallum autem, sonorum mutua unius ad alterum ratio, sive respectus.

**Русский.** Интервал же есть взаимное отношение, или соотнесённость, звуков — одного к другому.

**English.** An interval is the mutual ratio, or relation, of notes, of the one to the other.

*Notes.*

- RU: ratio, sive respectus: двойной перевод одного термина; respectus — «соотнесённость» (схоластическое употребление; ср. respectu в X18, in respectu в X38).  
  EN: ratio, sive respectus: a double rendering of one term; respectus is ‘relation’ in the scholastic sense (cf. respectu in X18, in respectu in X38).  
  Source: DMLBS, s.v. respectus, senses 4 (‘(phil.) relation’) and 5 (‘respectu, in respectu’ with the genitive: ‘as regards, with respect to; in comparison with’), via Logeion [checked]

**X10** · Xyl. 41.7–8

> **Latin.** Systema, complexus est certus intervallorum.

**Русский.** Система есть определённая совокупность интервалов.

**English.** A system is a determinate combination of intervals.

*Notes.*

- RU: complexus — букв. «охват, сплетение»; ср. обычное определение системы как составленного более чем из одного интервала.  
  EN: complexus is literally an ‘embracing, interweaving’; compare the usual definition of a system as that which is composed of more than one interval.  
  Source: Cleonides, Isagoge 1 (Jan) [from memory]

**X11** · Xyl. 41.8–9

> **Latin.** Harmonia, ipsa constructio systematum.

**Русский.** Гармония — само построение систем.

**English.** Harmony is the very construction of systems.

**X12** · Xyl. 41.9–13

> **Latin.** Enimvero sonus divisionis capax non est, ut neque punctum in Geometria. Harmoniae participem diximus, quia ita effertur, ut possit etiam cum alio sono ad harmoniam concinnari.

**Русский.** В самом деле, звук не допускает деления, как и точка в геометрии. Причастным же гармонии мы назвали его потому, что он издаётся так, что может быть слажен в гармонию и с другим звуком.

**English.** For indeed a note does not admit of division, any more than a point in geometry. We called it ‘partaking of harmony’ because it is uttered in such a way that it can also be framed into harmony with another note.

*Notes.*

- RU: Сравнение звука с точкой обычно в античной гармонике.  
  EN: The comparison of the note with the point is common in ancient harmonics.  
  Source: Nicomachus, Enchiridion 12 (Jan) [from memory]

**X13** · Xyl. 41.13–16

> **Latin.** Quod nisi fiat, iam is dissonus sit sonus, non consonus: imo neque sonus dicetur esse, sed strepitus quispiam temere editus, et ad consonantiam nulli existens usui.

**Русский.** Если же этого не будет, то такой звук окажется уже диссонирующим, а не консонирующим; более того, его и звуком не назовут, а неким шумом, изданным наугад и совершенно бесполезным для консонанса.

**English.** Should this not be so, that note would then be dissonant, not consonant; indeed it will not even be called a note, but some noise uttered at random and of no use for consonance.

*Notes.*

- RU: dissonus / consonus здесь означают «непригодный / пригодный к мелодии», а не консонанс в узком смысле X31–33.  
  EN: dissonus / consonus here mean ‘unfit / fit for melody’, not consonance in the narrow sense of X31–33.  
  Source: X31–33 of this text [checked]

**X14** · Xyl. 41.16–25

> **Latin.** Proinde diversae sunt sonorum, quas illi progressu et ordine, quem a gravi ad acutum observant, nanciscuntur nomenclationes: Proslambanomenos, Hypate hypaton, parhypate hypaton, hypate diatonos, hypate meson, parhypate meson, diatonos meson, mese monos, paramesos, trite diezeugmene, diatonos diezeugmene, nete diezeugmene, trite hyperbolaeon, diatonos hyperbolaeon, nete hyperbolaeon.

**Русский.** Соответственно различны и наименования звуков, которые те получают сообразно продвижению и порядку, соблюдаемому ими от низкого к высокому: просламбаномен, гипата низших, парипата низших, гипата диатоническая, гипата средних, парипата средних, диатоническая средних, меса монос, парамес, трита отделённая, диатоническая отделённая, нета отделённая, трита высших, диатоническая высших, нета высших.

**English.** Accordingly, the names of the notes are various, which they acquire by the progression and order that they keep from low to high: proslambanomenos, hypate hypaton, parhypate hypaton, hypate diatonos, hypate meson, parhypate meson, diatonos meson, mese monos, paramesos, trite diezeugmene, diatonos diezeugmene, nete diezeugmene, trite hyperbolaion, diatonos hyperbolaion, nete hyperbolaion.

*Checks.* 15 names are listed, the fifteen notes of the two-octave (Greater Perfect) system; the tetrachord synemmenon is absent.

*Notes.*

- RU: Пятнадцать звуков двухоктавной системы. Отличия от обычной номенклатуры (у Боэция): четвёртый звук назван hypate diatonos вместо lichanos hypaton (у Боэция в диатоническом роде — «lichanos hypaton diatonos»); «диатоническая» (diatonos) везде заменяет lichanos / paranete (у Боэция также «diatonos meson», «diatonos diezeugmenon»); у месы стоит добавление monos (греч. «один, единственный», в мужском роде при женском mese) — вероятно, в том смысле, что меса одна не носит имени тетрахорда; название тетрахорда diezeugmene стоит в единственном числе вместо diezeugmenon. Nomenclatio — редкое слово (Колумелла).  
  EN: The fifteen notes of the two-octave system. Departures from the usual nomenclature (as in Boethius): the fourth note is called hypate diatonos instead of lichanos hypaton (in Boethius, in the diatonic genus, ‘lichanos hypaton diatonos’); diatonos everywhere replaces lichanos / paranete (Boethius too has ‘diatonos meson’, ‘diatonos diezeugmenon’); mese carries the addition monos (Greek ‘alone, single’, masculine beside the feminine mese), presumably in the sense that mese alone bears no tetrachord name; the tetrachord name diezeugmene stands in the singular instead of diezeugmenon. Nomenclatio is a rare word (Columella).  
  Source: Boethius, De institutione musica, ed. Friedlein, read in the Latin Wikisource transcription of that edition, 1.20 and 1.22 [checked]; Lewis & Short, s.v. nomenclatio (Col. 3, 2, 31), via Logeion [checked]; page image of p. 41 for ‘mese monos’ [checked]

*Doubts.*

- *mese monos*
  - Issue: monos is not part of the usual name of mese
  - Options: keep as a name (‘mese monos’) / translate (‘mese alone’, ‘the single mese’)
  - Choice: kept as printed, since the convention is to keep the names as the version gives them; explained in the note
  - Evidence: page image p. 41, line 22 [checked]
  - Alternative (RU): меса одиночная
  - Alternative (EN): the single mese

**X15** · Xyl. 41.25–42.5

> **Latin.** Per has sonorum species heccaedecachordum, hoc est, sedecim chordarum instrumentum in quindecim fidibus instructo organo (Pentecaedecachordum vocant Graeci) absolvitur, fine octachordi ad principium sequentium continuo assumpto.

**Русский.** Этими видами звуков геккайдекахорд, то есть инструмент о шестнадцати струнах, осуществляется на инструменте, снабжённом пятнадцатью струнами (греки называют его пентекайдекахордом), поскольку конец октахорда тотчас принимается за начало последующих.

**English.** Through these species of notes the hekkaidekachord, that is, the instrument of sixteen strings, is completed on an instrument furnished with fifteen strings (the Greeks call it a pentekaidekachord), the end of the octachord being taken up directly as the beginning of those that follow.

*Checks.* Two octachords: 8 + 8 = 16 notes; the eighth note is shared, so 16 − 1 = 15 strings. Consistent with the 15 names of X14.

*Notes.*

- RU: heccaedecachordum, pentecaedecachordum — греческие слова в латинской одежде («шестнадцатиструнный», «пятнадцатиструнный»), переданы транслитерацией; «hoc est, sedecim chordarum instrumentum» — пояснение в самом тексте. Смысл: два октахорда дали бы шестнадцать звуков, но восьмой звук служит одновременно концом первого и началом второго, поэтому струн пятнадцать. Для струны у Ксиландра три слова: chorda, fides (здесь), nervus (X29–30, X41–42); все переведены «струна».  
  EN: heccaedecachordum, pentecaedecachordum are Greek words in Latin dress (‘sixteen-stringed’, ‘fifteen-stringed’), rendered by transliteration; hoc est, sedecim chordarum instrumentum is a gloss in the text itself. The sense: two octachords would give sixteen notes, but the eighth note serves at once as the end of the first and the beginning of the second, so the strings are fifteen. Xylander has three words for a string: chorda, fides (here), nervus (X29–30, X41–42); all are rendered ‘string’.  
  Source: Greek hekkaideka, pentekaideka + chorde; the compounds were looked for as headwords in LSJ via Logeion and not found there [checked]

*Doubts.*

- *fine octachordi ad principium sequentium continuo assumpto*
  - Issue: sequentium: of the following notes, strings or octachord; continuo: adverb (‘immediately’) or adjective
  - Options: ‘taken up directly as the beginning of those that follow’ / ‘taken in continuity to the beginning of the following [strings]’
  - Choice: ‘directly ... of those that follow’, leaving the noun open as the Latin does; continuo is the adverb: the printing marks it with the grave accent of adverbs (continuò)
  - Evidence: printed line 42.5 in the diplomatic transcription of the data file [checked]; Lewis & Short, s.v. continuus, adv. continuo (‘immediately, forthwith’), via Logeion [checked]
- *heccaedecachordum ... in quindecim fidibus instructo organo ... absolvitur*
  - Issue: the sentence says that a sixteen-string instrument is realised on a fifteen-string one
  - Options: as translated
  - Choice: rendered as it stands; the ablative absolute gives the explanation (the shared eighth note)
  - Evidence: arithmetic in checks [checked]

**X16** · Xyl. 42.5–7

> **Latin.** Quam enim rationem habet primus sonus ad octavum, eandem octavus ad decimumquintum obtinet.

**Русский.** Ибо какое отношение имеет первый звук к восьмому, такое же восьмой имеет к пятнадцатому.

**English.** For the ratio that the first note has to the eighth, the eighth holds to the fifteenth.

*Checks.* 1st : 8th = octave, 2:1; 8th : 15th = octave, 2:1; 1st : 15th = 4:1 (cf. X36).

**X18** · Xyl. 42.8–15 · ¶ 4

> **Latin.** Intervallorum autem minimum genus est, quod diesin vocant: deinde semitonium, cuius dupla ad acutum est accessio, respectu dieseos. Deinde est tonus duplici constans semitonio, et rationem sesquioctavam constituens, ut est novenarii ad octonarium numerum. **Русский.** Наименьший же род интервалов — тот, что называют диесой; затем идёт полутон, продвижение которого к высокому вдвое больше по сравнению с диесой. Затем идёт тон, состоящий из двух полутонов и образующий сверхосминное [9:8] отношение, каково отношение числа девять к числу восемь. Отсюда получается, что полутон есть как бы половина сверхосминного [9:8], а диеса — как бы его четверть.

**English.** Of intervals, the smallest kind is that which they call the diesis; next the semitone, whose advance toward the high is double in comparison with the diesis. Next is the tone, consisting of two semitones and constituting the sesquioctave [9:8] ratio, as is that of the number nine to the number eight. Whence it comes about that the semitone is, as it were, half of the sesquioctave [9:8], and the diesis, as it were, a quarter of the same.

*Checks.* Tone 9:8 = 203.91 cents (1200·log2(9/8)). Its exact half is √(9/8) ≈ 1.06066 (101.96 cents), its quarter (9/8)^(1/4) ≈ 1.02988 (50.98 cents); neither is a ratio of whole numbers, hence the text's ‘quasi’. Diesis = 1/4 tone, semitone = 2 dieses = 1/2 tone, tone = 2 semitones: consistent.

*Notes.*

- RU: Диеса здесь — четверть тона (энгармоническая диеса); величины по Аристоксену (тон = 2 полутона = 4 диесы), но тон определён пифагорейски, отношением 9:8. Dupla ... accessio — обычное «вдвое», а не название отношения, поэтому без скобок.  
  EN: The diesis is here the quarter-tone (the enharmonic diesis); the magnitudes are Aristoxenian (tone = 2 semitones = 4 dieses), but the tone is defined in the Pythagorean way by the ratio 9:8. The word quasi (‘as it were’) marks that the half and the quarter of 9:8 cannot be expressed in numbers; this is argued in X19–25. Boethius, on the contrary, proves that the tone cannot be divided into two equal parts. Dupla ... accessio is an ordinary ‘double’, not a ratio name, and so has no bracket.  
  Source: Boethius, De institutione musica 3.1 and 1.16–17, ed. Friedlein; Euclid, Sectio canonis, prop. 16; Cleonides, Isagoge 2 (Jan) [from memory]; Lewis & Short, s.v. accessio (‘approach; increase’), via Logeion [checked]

*Doubts.*

- *cuius dupla ad acutum est accessio, respectu dieseos*

  - Options: ‘whose advance toward the high is double’ / ‘whose increase toward the high is double’
  - Choice: ‘advance’: movement in pitch toward the high (ad acutum)
  - Evidence: Lewis & Short, s.v. accessio, via Logeion [checked]

**X32** · Xyl. 45.2–13

> **Latin.** Inter paraphonum et antiphonum, hoc est assonans et obsonans, hoc differt, quod illud quidem consonat inaequali tempore, quando soni mutuas suas proportionalitates atque rationes aequalitate quadam leniter et concinne sibi invicem succedunt. Antiphonum autem est, cum simul eodem tempore acutum cum gravi consonat: ut cum octavus primo, undecimus quarto, duodecimus quinto, decimusquintus octavo aequaliter ascendit vel descendit in intensionibus remissionibusve, ita ut acutis graves, vel contra, respondeant proportionaliter.

**Русский.** Между парафонным и антифонным, то есть «звучащим при» и «звучащим навстречу», разница та, что первое консонирует в неравное время, когда звуки, [храня] взаимные свои пропорциональности и отношения, с некоторой равномерностью мягко и складно сменяют друг друга. Антифонное же — это когда высокое консонирует с низким вместе, в одно и то же время: например, когда восьмой [звук] с первым, одиннадцатый с четвёртым, двенадцатый с пятым, пятнадцатый с восьмым равномерно восходит или нисходит в повышениях или понижениях, так что низкие [звуки] высоким, или наоборот, отвечают пропорционально.

**English.** Between the paraphonon and the antiphonon, that is, the ‘sounding to’ and the ‘sounding over against’, the difference is this: the former is consonant at unequal time, when the notes, [keeping] their mutual proportionalities and ratios, succeed one another gently and neatly with a certain evenness. The antiphonon, on the other hand, is when high is consonant with low together, at the same time: as when the eighth [note] rises or falls evenly with the first, the eleventh with the fourth, the twelfth with the fifth, the fifteenth with the eighth, in raisings or lowerings, so that low [notes] answer to high, or the reverse, proportionally.

*Checks.* 8th–1st, 11th–4th, 12th–5th, 15th–8th: each pair is seven steps apart, i.e. an octave (2:1). None of the pairs is an octave and a fourth (1st–11th, 8:3), an octave and a fifth (1st–12th, 3:1) or a double octave (1st–15th, 4:1), although X31 counts these as antiphonic. The numbers 11, 12 and 15 are the note-counts of those three intervals.

*Notes.*

- RU: Assonans, obsonans — новые латинские пояснения к тем же двум терминам (в X31 — iuxta sonans, contra sonans); assonare в классической латыни — «отзываться звуком», obsonare — «перебивать звуком» (Плавт), здесь оба взяты как кальки («звучащее при», «звучащее навстречу»). Различие по Ксиландру: парафонные консонансы звучат последовательно («в неравное время»), антифонные — одновременно, причём голоса движутся параллельно. В латинском тексте три слова одного корня: inaequali (tempore), aequalitate (quadam), aequaliter; в переводе — «неравное», «равномерность», «равномерно». Все четыре примера — октавы; числа 11, 12 и 15 совпадают с числом звуков октавы с квартой, октавы с квинтой и двойной октавы, но в тексте они сопоставлены не с первым звуком.  
  EN: Assonans, obsonans are fresh Latin glosses on the same two terms (in X31 iuxta sonans, contra sonans); in classical Latin assonare is ‘to sound in response’, obsonare ‘to interrupt with sound’ (Plautus), and both are used here as calques (‘sounding to’, ‘sounding over against’). The difference according to Xylander: paraphonic consonances sound in succession (‘at unequal time’), antiphonic ones simultaneously, the voices moving in parallel. The Latin has three words of one root: inaequali (tempore), aequalitate (quadam), aequaliter; the translation has ‘unequal’, ‘evenness’, ‘evenly’. All four examples are octaves; the numbers 11, 12 and 15 are the note-counts of the octave and a fourth, the octave and a fifth and the double octave, but the text does not pair them with the first note.  
  Source: Lewis & Short, s.vv. assono, obsono (2), via Logeion [checked]; Ps.-Aristotle, Problems 19.39 [from memory]

**X34** · Xyl. 45.16–24

> **Latin.** Conficitur autem diatessaron duobus ex tonis, unoque, semitonio, et praeterea quopiam exiguo: quod quia minus diesi est, appellationem peculiarem non invenit. Diapente vero ex tribus tonis, semitonioque constat. Diapason autem ex sex tonis conficitur. Diapason autem cum diatessaron, ex octo tonis et semitonio conflatur. Diapason cum diapente, ex novem tonis et semitonio. Disdiapason, ex duodecim tonis.

**Русский.** Составляется же кварта из двух тонов, одного полутона и, сверх того, чего-то малого, что, будучи меньше диесы, не получило особого названия. Квинта же состоит из трёх тонов и полутона. **English.** Now the fourth is made up of two tones and one semitone, and besides of some small thing which, because it is less than a diesis, has found no name of its own. The fifth consists of three tones and a semitone. The octave is made up of six tones. The octave and a fourth is composed of eight tones and a semitone. The octave and a fifth, of nine tones and a semitone. The double octave, of twelve tones.

*Checks.* In tones (Aristoxenian reckoning): 2½ + 3½ = 6; 6 + 2½ = 8½; 6 + 3½ = 9½; 6 + 6 = 12: the sums agree, except that the ‘small something’ added to the fourth is not carried into the larger intervals. By ratios, with the tone 9:8 = 203.91 cents and the semitone as its half (101.96): fourth 4:3 = 498.04 cents, but 2½ tones = 509.78, so the fourth is smaller than 2½ tones by 11.73 cents, not larger; fifth 3:2 = 701.96 against 3½ tones = 713.69; octave 2:1 = 1200 against 6 tones = 1223.46 (excess 23.46 cents, the Pythagorean comma 531441:524288).

*Notes.*

- RU: Счёт в тонах и полутонах — аристоксеновский. Добавка «чего-то малого, меньшего диесы» к кварте стоит особняком: по отношениям кварта [4:3], напротив, меньше двух с половиной тонов (остаток после двух тонов — 256:243, меньше половины тона), а шесть тонов больше октавы.   EN: The reckoning in tones and semitones is Aristoxenian. The addition of ‘some small thing, less than a diesis’ to the fourth stands alone: by ratios the fourth [4:3] is, on the contrary, smaller than two and a half tones (what remains after two tones is 256:243, less than half a tone), and six tones exceed the octave. Boethius says just the opposite: ‘Tota enim diatessaron consonantia duorum tonorum est ac semitonii, sed non pleni’ (‘the whole fourth consists of two tones and a semitone, but not a full one’). In X47 the same fourth consists simply of two tones and a semitone. What the Latin says has been translated.  
  Source: Boethius, De institutione musica, ed. Friedlein, read in the Latin Wikisource transcription of that edition, 1.21 (the sentence quoted), 1.19 (‘Diapason quinque tonis et duobus semitoniis iungi’) and 2.31 (‘... quoniam diapason sex tonis non constet’) (the text of 1.21, the headings of 1.19 and 2.31) [checked]; Boethius 3.1–3; Euclid, Sectio canonis, props. 14–15; Cleonides, Isagoge 8 (Jan) (the chapter number in Cleonides is not verified) [from memory]

*Notes.*

  Source: page image, p. 43 [checked]; LSJ s.v. πεντεκαιδεκάχορδος [from memory]

*Doubts.*

- *Ex his conficitur, quod in Pentekaedecachordo est Heckaedecachordum*
  - Issue: quod may be the relative pronoun, with Heckaedecachordum as its antecedent drawn into the clause ('the sixteen-stringed that is in the fifteen-stringed is made up'), or a conjunction after conficitur ('it follows that …').
  - Options: (a) 'From these is made up the sixteen-stringed [system] that is in the fifteen-stringed'; (b) 'From this it follows that in the fifteen-stringed [system] there is a sixteen-stringed one'.
  - Choice: (a): conficere is used of composition throughout this text (V32, V39, V55, V65), ex his (plural) naturally means the fifteen notes just listed, and the classical construction for 'it follows that' is ex quo conficitur ut. The doctrine is the same in both.

  - Alternative (RU): Из этого следует, что в пятнадцатиструнной [системе] есть шестнадцатиструнная…
  - Alternative (EN): From this it follows that in the fifteen-stringed [system] there is a sixteen-stringed one…

**V22** · Vinet 44.6–14 · ¶ III.1

> **Latin.** Iam vero intervallorum minimum quidem est Diesis. Deinde hemitonium, quod Diesi duplo acutius est: Postremo Tonus, ex duobus constans hemitoniis: cuius quidem intervalli ratio sesquioctava est, quomodo novem numerus ad octo habet: ut sit Hemitonium, veluti octo cum semisse: et Diesis, ut, octo cum quadrante:

**Русский.** Что же до интервалов, то наименьший из них — диеса. Затем полутон, который вдвое выше диесы; наконец, тон, состоящий из двух полутонов; отношение этого интервала — сверхосминное [9:8], как число девять относится к восьми; так что полутон — это как бы восемь с половиной, а диеса — как бы восемь с четвертью;

**English.** Now of the intervals the smallest is the diesis. Next the semitone, which is twice as high as the diesis; and last the tone, consisting of two semitones; the ratio of this interval is sesquioctave [9:8], as the number nine stands to eight; so that the semitone is, as it were, eight and a half, and the diesis, as it were, eight and a quarter;

*Checks.* Tone 9:8 = 203.91 cents (cents = 1200 · log2 of the ratio). 'Eight and a half' to eight = 17:16 = 104.96 cents; 'eight and a quarter' to eight = 33:32 = 53.27 cents. This halves the excess of 9 over 8 arithmetically, not the interval: (17/16)² = 289/256, greater than 9/8 = 288/256; (33/32)² = 1089/1024, greater than 17/16 = 1088/1024. The true half of the tone is 8 : √72 ≈ 8 : 8.485 (101.96 cents), the quarter ≈ 50.98 cents. The text itself says veluti ('as it were').

*Notes.*

- RU: Hemitonium — греческая форма (у Ксиландра semitonium); в переводе в обоих случаях «полутон». Диеса здесь — четверть тона (половина полутона), как в гармоническом роде (V59).  
  EN: Hemitonium is the Greek form (Xylander has semitonium); both are 'semitone' in translation. The diesis here is the quarter-tone (half of the semitone), as in the harmonic genus (V59).  
  Source: latin-texts.json, X18 [checked]; Boethius, De institutione musica I, 21, ed. Friedlein (1867), text of the archive.org scan, p. 213 ('diesis autem est semitonii dimidium') [checked]
- RU: «Diesi duplo acutius» — буквально «вдвое выше диесы»: полутон есть вдвое больший шаг вверх, чем диеса. «Восемь с половиной» и «восемь с четвертью» — члены, которые вместе с восемью дают полутон и диесу (17:16 и 33:32); это деление избытка, а не интервала пополам, и точным оно быть не может: сверхчастичное отношение не делится на две равные части в числах. У Боэция 17:16 (sesquisextadecima) — бо́льшая из двух неравных частей тона 18:17:16, превосходящая его половину.  
  EN: Diesi duplo acutius: literally 'higher than the diesis by double', i.e. the semitone is twice as large a step upward as the diesis. 'Eight and a half' and 'eight and a quarter' are the terms which with eight give the semitone and the diesis (17:16 and 33:32); this halves the excess, not the interval, and cannot be exact: a superparticular ratio cannot be divided into two equal parts in numbers. In Boethius 17:16 (the sesquisextadecima) is the larger of the two unequal parts of the tone 18:17:16 and exceeds its half.  
  Source: Boethius, De institutione musica III, 1, ed. Friedlein (1867), text of the archive.org scan, p. 268–271 (the terms 16, 17, 18) [checked]; Euclid, Sectio canonis, prop. 3 [from memory]

*Doubts.*

- *ut sit Hemitonium, veluti octo cum semisse: et Diesis, ut, octo cum quadrante*
  - Issue: The Latin names only the larger term; the second term (eight) is understood from the preceding 'novem ad octo'.
  - Options: (a) semitone = 8½ (to 8), diesis = 8¼ (to 8); (b) 'eight with a half [of a unit of the tone]' in some looser sense.
  - Choice: (a), rendered literally without supplying the second term.

**V37** · Vinet 47.21–48.7 · ¶ III.7

> **Latin.** Differunt autem inter se Paraphonum et antiphonum, quod Paraphonum impari temporis spatio consonat, leniter quodammodo et apposite excipientibus inter se sonis, secundum proportiones et rationes aequabiliter: antiphonum vero aequo convenit temporis spacio, acuto cum gravi pariter consonante, ut octavo cum primo, undecimo cum quarto, duodecimo cum quinto, quintodecimo cum octavo, gravibus nimirum una cum acutis, aut acutis cum gravibus peraequa proportione simul ascendentibus vel descendentibus, in intensionibus remissionibusve.

> **Latin.** quandoquidem intervallum Dia-pente, cuius est ratio sesquialtera, sesquialterum minime videtur ex ratione numeri sonorum, quo numero superat sonos, qui ipsum praecedunt. **English.** since the interval of the fifth, whose ratio is sesquialter [3:2], by no means appears sesquialter [3:2] on a reckoning by the number of the notes, the number by which it surpasses the notes that precede it. *Checks.* The fifth taken as the number 5: 5:1 quintuple; 5:2 = 2½, duple sesquialter; 5:3 = 1⅔, superpartient (exactly superbipartiens tertias); 5:4 sesquiquartan. None equals 3:2. Correct.

*Notes.*

Названия отношений, которых нет в таблице проекта, образованы по тому же образцу: duplum sesquialterum (у Боэция duplex sesqualter) — «двукратное полуторное [5:2]»; superpartiens — «сверхчастное [5:3]» (у Вине родовое имя, без уточнения; точное — superbipartiens tertias).  
  EN: The 'interval of the fifth' is here taken as the fifth note with its ordinal number 5, which is compared with the numbers 1–4. Ex ratione numeri sonorum: 'on a reckoning by the number of the notes'; ratio here is not 'ratio' but 'reckoning, account'. The genitives (primi soni quincuplex and so on) mean 'the quintuple of the first note'. The ratio names not in the project's table are formed on the same pattern: duplum sesquialterum (in Boethius duplex sesqualter), 'duple sesquialter [5:2]'; superpartiens, 'superpartient [5:3]' (in Vinet the generic name, unspecified; the exact name is superbipartiens tertias).  
  Source: Boethius, De institutione arithmetica I, 28–29 and 31, ed. Friedlein (1867), text of the archive.org scan, p. 57–65 (superpartiens; duplex sesqualter, p. 61; superbipartiens, p. 60) [checked]; Lewis & Short s.v. ratio ('a reckoning, account, calculation'), read at lsj.gr [checked]; about.md, § 4.3 [checked]

> **Latin.** quinimo, quoniam ex tribus intervallis Dia-tessaron intervallum confectum est, duobusque tonis et hemitonio comprehensum, necesse est, postquam duo prima intervalla imposueris, reliquum esse Hemitonium:

**Русский.** напротив, поскольку интервал кварты составлен из трёх интервалов и охватывается двумя тонами и полутоном, то, когда положишь два первых интервала, остающийся по необходимости будет полутоном;

**English.** rather, since the interval of the fourth is made up of three intervals and is comprised by two tones and a semitone, it is necessary that, once you have laid down the first two intervals, the remaining one be a semitone;

*Checks.* Diatonic tetrachord: tone + tone + semitone = 2½ tones. Here the fourth is exactly two tones and a semitone, without the 'small part' of V39.

*Notes.*

- RU: «Два первых интервала» — два тона; какими должны быть эти два интервала, латинский текст не говорит, это видно из V53 и V56.  
  EN: The 'first two intervals' are the two tones; the Latin does not say what these two intervals must be, it is clear from V53 and V56.  
  Source: the Latin text, V53, V56 [checked]

**V56** · Vinet 52.6–8

> **Latin.** atque hoc fuerit Dia-tessaron Diatonon cognominatum genus, ex Hemitonio et tonis constans duobus.

**Русский.** и это будет род кварты, называемый диатоническим, состоящий из полутона и двух тонов.

**English.** and this will be the genus of the fourth called diatonic, consisting of a semitone and two tones.

*Checks.* ½ + 1 + 1 = 2½ tones.

*Notes.*

- RU: Обычный порядок диатонического тетрахорда от низкого звука: полутон, тон, тон.  
  EN: The usual order of the diatonic tetrachord from the lowest note: semitone, tone, tone.  
  Source: Boethius, De institutione musica I, 21, ed. Friedlein (1867), text of the archive.org scan, p. 213 ('per semitonium, tonum et tonum') [checked]

**V57** · Vinet 52.9–11

> **Latin.** Si vero duo sint hemitonia, reliquum fuerit unum ex confusione intervallum, Trihemitonium simplex:

**Русский.** Если же полутонов два, остатком будет один интервал, [возникший] из слияния, — простой трёхполутон;

**English.** But if there are two semitones, the remainder will be one interval [arising] from blending, a simple trihemitone;

*Checks.* 2½ − (½ + ½) = 1½ tones = a trihemitone.

*Notes.*

- RU: «Ex confusione» — «из слияния»: три полутона слиты в один шаг (ср. «coniunctis confusisque» в V32). «Простой» — несоставной, см. V31.  
  EN: Ex confusione, 'from blending': three semitones are fused into a single step (compare 'coniunctis confusisque' in V32). 'Simple' means incomposite, see V31.  
  Source: the Latin text, V31–V32 [checked]

**V58** · Vinet 52.11–14

> **Latin.** eritque id Dia-tessaron genus Chromaticum, ex Hemitoniis duobus et Trihemitonio temperatum, quod priore moestius luctuosiusque est.

**Русский.** и это будет хроматический род кварты, слаженный из двух полутонов и трёхполутона; он печальнее и скорбнее предыдущего.

**English.** and that will be the chromatic genus of the fourth, tempered out of two semitones and a trihemitone, which is sadder and more mournful than the former.

*Checks.* ½ + ½ + 1½ = 2½ tones.

*Notes.*

- RU: Temperatum — «смешанный в должной мере, слаженный» (не в позднейшем смысле музыкальной темперации); тот же глагол в V60. Характер хроматического рода как жалобного — общее место; у Боэция хроматика «мягче» диатоники.  
  EN: Temperatum: 'mixed in due measure, attuned' (not in the later sense of musical temperament); the same verb in V60. The plaintive character of the chromatic genus is a commonplace; in Boethius the chromatic is 'softer' than the diatonic.  
  Source: Lewis & Short s.v. tempero I ('to divide or proportion duly, mingle in due proportion; to combine or compound properly'), read at lsj.gr [checked]; Boethius, De institutione musica I, 21, ed. Friedlein (1867), text of the archive.org scan, p. 212–213 ('in mollius decidens') [checked]

**V59** · Vinet 52.14–20

> **Latin.** Si vero per duas dieses modulatio peragatur, quod reliquum est intervallum, erit unum ditonium ex confusione, ac Dia-tessaron hoc genus Harmonicum nominatur, quod sit optimum, ab ipsa communi omnium harmonia cognominatum.

**Русский.** Если же мелодия ведётся через две диесы, то остающийся интервал будет одним дитоном, [возникшим] из слияния, и этот род кварты называется гармоническим: как наилучший, он назван по самой гармонии, общей для всех.

**English.** But if the melody is carried through two dieses, the interval that remains will be one ditone [arising] from blending, and this genus of the fourth is named harmonic: because it is the best, it has been named after harmony itself, which is common to all.

*Checks.* ¼ + ¼ + 2 = 2½ tones; the diesis is a quarter-tone (V22), the ditone two tones.

*Notes.*

- RU: Объяснение названия: род назван именем самой гармонии, общим для всех родов, потому что он лучший из них. У Боэция энармонический род — «optime atque apte coniunctum» («наилучшим и подобающим образом слаженный»).  
  EN: The explanation of the name: the genus bears the name of harmony itself, common to all the genera, because it is the best of them. In Boethius the enharmonic genus is the one 'optime atque apte coniunctum' (best and most fitly joined).  
  Source: Boethius, De institutione musica I, 21, ed. Friedlein (1867), text of the archive.org scan, p. 213 [checked]

*Doubts.*

- *ab ipsa communi omnium harmonia cognominatum*
  - Issue: omnium: 'of all [the genera]' or 'of all things'.
  - Options: (a) 'after harmony itself, common to all [the genera]'; (b) 'after the common harmony of all things'.
  - Choice: Left as 'common to all', which the Latin allows either way; (a) fits the context.
  - Evidence: the Latin text, V52 [checked]

**English.** In the same way harmony constitutes and tempers the other systems as well.

**V61** · Vinet 52.23–27

**Русский.** Ведь из предложенного примера, в котором на одном тетрахорде показано, как устраиваются интервалы интервала кварты, усердный [читатель] легко сможет понять, как составляются другие системы.

**English.** For from the example set forth, by which it has been shown in a single tetrachord how the intervals of the interval of the fourth are constituted, the studious [reader] will easily be able to grasp how the other systems are composed.

**V62** · Vinet 53.1–4 · ¶ V.4

> **Latin.** Genus autem Harmonicum est modulatu difficillimum, meditatione multa exercitationeque indigens: unde eius est usus rarior.

**Русский.** Гармонический же род труднее всех в мелодическом исполнении и требует долгой подготовки и упражнения; оттого он и употребляется реже.

**English.** The harmonic genus, however, is the hardest to render in melody and needs much practice and exercise; hence its use is rarer.

*Notes.*

- RU: Modulatu — супин от modulari («петь, играть размеренно»): «труден для мелодического исполнения». Meditatio здесь не «размышление», а «подготовка, упражнение» (цицероновская пара meditatio atque exercitatio).  
  EN: Modulatu is the supine of modulari ('to sing or play in measure'): 'hard to perform as melody'. Meditatio here is not 'reflection' but 'preparation, practice' (the Ciceronian pair meditatio atque exercitatio).  
  Source: Lewis & Short s.v. meditatio II.A (Cic. Div. 2, 96) and s.v. modulor II ('to measure rhythmically; to modulate; to sing, to play'), read at lsj.gr [checked]

**V63** · Vinet 53.4–6

> **Latin.** Diatonum vero est simplum et generosum et magis naturale: quare id et Plato admittit.

**Русский.** Диатонический же прост, благороден и более естествен; поэтому его и Платон принимает.

**English.** The diatonic, on the other hand, is simple and noble and more natural; and for that reason Plato too admits it.

*Notes.*

- RU: Simplum — редкая форма (как прилагательное засвидетельствована лишь в поздней латыни), отличная от simplex, которым Вине называет несоставные интервалы (V31). Generosum — «благородный». Форма Diatonum рядом с Diatonon в V52–V56.  
  EN: Simplum is a rare form (as an adjective attested only in late Latin), distinct from simplex, by which Vinet names the incomposite intervals (V31). Generosum: 'noble'. The form Diatonum stands beside Diatonon in V52–V56.  
  Source: Lewis & Short and Gaffiot s.v. simplus (Prudentius), read at lsj.gr [checked]
- RU: О Платоне: по-видимому, имеется в виду диатоническое деление мировой души в «Тимее» (с остатком 256:243); текст места не называет. У Боэция диатонический род «aliquanto durius et naturalius» («несколько твёрже и естественнее»).  
  EN: On Plato: the diatonic division of the world-soul in the Timaeus (with the remainder 256:243) is presumably meant; the text names no passage. In Boethius the diatonic genus is 'aliquanto durius et naturalius' (somewhat harder and more natural).  
  Source: Plato, Timaeus 35b–36b [from memory]; Boethius, De institutione musica I, 21, ed. Friedlein (1867), text of the archive.org scan, p. 212 [checked]

**V64** · Vinet 53.7–14 · ¶ V.5

> **Latin.** Horum autem unumquodque rursus variatur, et in multas divisiones et differentias et genera modulationum distribuitur: et modo Lydius, modo Phrygius, modo Dorius appellatur modulus: a quorum quolibet Paeeon, Hymenaeus, Exodius, et quotquot sunt modulationum genera, existunt.

**Русский.** Каждый же из этих [родов], в свою очередь, видоизменяется и распределяется по многим делениям, различиям и родам мелодий; и лад называется то лидийским, то фригийским, то дорийским; а от каждого из них происходят пеан, гименей, эксодий и все, сколько их есть, роды мелодий.

**English.** Each of these, in turn, is varied and distributed into many divisions and differences and genera of melodies; and the mode is called now Lydian, now Phrygian, now Dorian; and from each of these arise the paean, the hymenaeus, the exodius, and all the genera of melodies that there are.

*Notes.*

- RU: Modulus у Вине — лад (ср. у Плиния: moduli Lydii, Dorii, Phrygii); слово значит также «мера, напев». Genera («роды») употреблено здесь дважды уже не о трёх родах тетрахорда, а о видах песен.  
  EN: Modulus in Vinet is the mode (compare Pliny: moduli Lydii, Dorii, Phrygii); the word also means 'measure, melody'. Genera is used here twice, no longer of the three genera of the tetrachord but of kinds of song.  
  Source: Lewis & Short s.v. modulus I.4 (Plin. 7, 204), read at lsj.gr [checked]
- RU: Пеан — песнь Аполлону, гименей — свадебная песнь; Exodius — «исходная», песнь при выходе или заключении (ср. лат. exodium — заключительная пьеса, «конец»). Напечатано Pæéon — передача греческой формы παιήων; обычная латинская форма — Paean.  
  EN: The paean is a song to Apollo, the hymenaeus a wedding song; Exodius is a song 'of going out', for an exit or conclusion (compare Latin exodium, an afterpiece, 'end'). The print has Pæéon, which transliterates the Greek form παιήων; the usual Latin form is Paean.  
  Source: Lewis & Short s.v. exodium, read at lsj.gr [checked]; Lewis & Short s.v. Paean, s.v. Hymenaeus [from memory]; page image, p. 53, line 12 [checked]

*Doubts.*

- *a quorum quolibet … existunt*
  - Issue: quorum may refer to the three modes just named or, more loosely, to 'these' genera.
  - Options: (a) 'from each of these [modes]'; (b) 'from each of these [genera]'.
  - Choice: (a): the nearest masculine or neuter antecedent is the three moduli; 'these' in translation keeps the reference to what immediately precedes.
  - Evidence: the Latin text [checked]

**V65** · Vinet 53.14–20

> **Latin.** Non enim sola intervallorum mutatione, neque sola eorum differentia: sed etiam lentioribus, et concitatis transitionibus, pausis quoque et fluxibus, atque variis tactibus pulsibusque, pro vario manus temperamento, infinitae et diversae modulationes conficiuntur.

**Русский.** Ибо не одной только переменой интервалов и не одним только их различием, но также более медленными и стремительными переходами, равно как паузами и течениями, и разнообразными касаниями и ударами, сообразно различной мере [движения] руки, создаются бесчисленные и разнообразные мелодии.

**English.** For it is not by change of the intervals alone, nor by their difference alone, but also by slower and by rapid transitions, by pauses too and flowings, and by various touches and strokes, according to the varying control of the hand, that infinite and diverse melodies are produced.

*Notes.*

- RU: Перечислены средства мелодии помимо интервалов: темп переходов от звука к звуку, остановки (pausae) и слитное движение (fluxus), способы касаться струн и ударять по ним (tactus, pulsus). Temperamentum — «соразмерение, мера»: то, как рука соразмеряет свои движения.  
  EN: The means of melody besides the intervals are listed: the speed of the passages from note to note, rests (pausae) and continuous movement (fluxus), the ways of touching and striking the strings (tactus, pulsus). Temperamentum: 'due mixing, measure', the way the hand proportions its movements.  
  Source: Lewis & Short s.v. temperamentum I, s.v. pausa ('a pause, halt, stop, cessation') and s.v. concito (concitatus A, 'rapid, swift, quick'), read at lsj.gr [checked]; Lewis & Short s.v. pulsus (pulsus lyrae) [from memory]

*Doubts.*

- *pro vario manus temperamento*
  - Issue: What temperamentum manus is: the control or regulation of the hand in playing, or its 'tempering' of the strings.
  - Options: (a) 'according to the varying control of the hand'; (b) 'according to the varied tuning by the hand'.
  - Choice: (a): the phrase qualifies the touches and strokes just named.
  - Evidence: Lewis & Short s.v. temperamentum ('a mixing in due proportion; measure, moderation'), read at lsj.gr [checked]
  - Alternative (RU): …сообразно различной настройке, [производимой] рукой…
  - Alternative (EN): …according to the varied tuning by the hand…
- *lentioribus, et concitatis transitionibus*
  - Issue: A comparative paired with a positive participle.
  - Options: 'slower and rapid transitions'.
  - Choice: Rendered as it stands, comparative and positive.
  - Evidence: the Latin text [checked]

## Text issues

**X18** (Xylander)

- Reading text: dimidium sesquioctavae diesis autem
- Printed: dimidium sesquioctauæ diesis autem (42.14)
- Proposal: punctuate: dimidium sesquioctavae, diesis autem
- Argument: A comma or colon is wanting between the two clauses.
- Effect on sense: None.

**X23** (Xylander)

- Reading text: Numerorum enim sedecim, qui est quadratus, neque in duo potest diducere quadrata
- Printed: Numerorum enim sedecim, qui est quadratus (43.3; confirmed on the page image)
- Proposal: Numerum enim sedecim, qui est quadratus
- Argument: diducere and dare need an accusative object, and the relative qui est quadratus refers to a masculine singular (numerus); the genitive plural Numerorum can only be construed as a loose partitive. Probably a slip of the compositor or of the translator (-orum for -um). The reading text keeps the printed form, and the translation follows it as a partitive.
- Effect on sense: None: ‘for the number sixteen, which is a square, it can neither part into two squares ...’ instead of ‘for, among numbers, sixteen ...’.

**X24** (Xylander)

- Reading text: quod propositum dupli esse constat
- Printed: quod propositū dupli esse cōstat (43.14; confirmed on the page image)
- Proposal: quod propositi duplum esse constat
- Argument: The parallel clause is ‘id statim propositi est dimidium’; the geometry requires that the square on the diagonal be double the given square. As printed, the endings of the two words seem to have been exchanged (propositum dupli for propositi duplum).
- Effect on sense: None for the translation, which follows the geometry: ‘which is agreed to be the double of the one proposed’. Taken strictly as printed, the clause would say that the proposed square is ‘of the double’, which gives no usable sense.

**X32** (Xylander)

- Reading text: quando soni mutuas suas proportionalitates atque rationes aequalitate quadam leniter et concinne sibi invicem succedunt
- Printed: as the reading text (45.5–7)
- Proposal: no change proposed; possibly a participle (servantes, retinentes) has dropped out after rationes
- Argument: The accusative mutuas suas proportionalitates atque rationes has no governing word, since succedunt is construed with sibi invicem. Either an accusative of respect or a lost participle.
- Effect on sense: Slight: ‘[keeping] their mutual proportionalities and ratios’ against ‘in respect of their mutual proportionalities and ratios’.

**X34** (Xylander)

- Reading text: duobus ex tonis, unoque, semitonio, et praeterea quopiam exiguo
- Printed: duobus ex tonis, unoqúe semitonio, et præterea quopiam exiguo (45.17: no comma after unoque, in the diplomatic transcription and on the page image)
- Proposal: unoque semitonio (no comma), as printed
- Argument: uno agrees with semitonio. The comma after unoque stands only in the reading text; the printing has the -que abbreviation with its accent and no comma, and the comma is not listed among the corrections.
- Effect on sense: None.

**X38** (Xylander)

- Reading text: ad excessum quantitatis respiciendo, qua in respectu se praecedentium sonorum habet
- Printed: as the reading text (46.15–17)
- Proposal: perhaps quam ... habet
- Argument: With qua the clause must be construed ‘in which [quantity] it stands’ (se habet), which leaves in respectu praecedentium sonorum without a pronoun for ‘it’; with quam, excessum (or quantitatem) ... habet has its object and se goes with praecedentium (‘the notes preceding it’). The abbreviation stroke of quā / quã may have been lost.
- Effect on sense: None: ‘the excess of quantity which it has with respect to the notes preceding it’ against ‘the excess of quantity in which it stands with respect to the preceding notes’.

**V17** (Vinet)

- Reading text: Mese solum
- Printed: Mése solùm (43.15)
- Proposal: Keep; solum is the adverb ('only'), a remark on the name rather than part of it.
- Argument: The grave accent marks the adverb in this printing; the note has a one-word name in the usual nomenclature.
- Effect on sense: If solum were taken as part of the name, the note would be called 'mese sola'; the translation has 'mese only' / «просто меса».

## Summary

Both Latin versions are translated in full, each directly from the Latin into Russian and into English: Xylander in 58 segments, Vinet in 66. The hardest places were the definition of the note (X08, V11), the sentence on the sixteen- and fifteen-stringed systems (X15, V18), the account of paraphonic and antiphonic consonance (X32, V37) and the passage denying that the ratio of the fifth can be read from the count of notes (X38, V44–45). The two versions differ most in vocabulary: Xylander writes incomposita, commensuratio, melodia and glosses the Greek names (quarta, quinta, octava; iuxta sonans, contra sonans, assonans, obsonans; decimaquinta), while Vinet writes simplicia beside non-composita, symmetria, convenientia and modulatio, keeps the Greek names without glosses and divides the text into chapters. They differ in sense at the division of the tone, where Vinet has the semitone 'as it were eight and a half' and the diesis 'eight and a quarter' and Xylander 'as it were half' and 'a quarter' of the sesquioctave; in V05, where music is called the Universe itself; and in the last section, where Vinet has modes (modulus) and Xylander tunes (cantilena). Every ratio and sum was recomputed: the examples on the number six are right in both versions, while the statement that the fourth is two tones, a semitone and 'something small besides' contradicts the ratios (4:3 is 11.73 cents less than two and a half tones) and Boethius 1.21; it is translated as it stands and explained in a note. Least sure are these: V18, where quod is taken as a relative ('the sixteen-stringed system that is in the fifteen-stringed one') with 'it follows that' given as the alternative; X32, where the Latin lacks a verb for mutuas proportionalitates atque rationes and [keeping] is supplied; V44 ex ratione numeri sonorum, rendered 'on a reckoning by the number of the notes'; V45 acuti and V49 intensio; X26 and V31 rhythmus; X57 tactus and V65 temperamentum manus. In the text, the comma in X34 'unoque, semitonio' stands only in the reading text and not in the printing, which is a matter for the data file; X23 Numerorum, X24 propositum dupli and X38 qua are suspect in the printing itself; Vinet's printing has an uncaptioned diagram of string lengths on p. 49 that the segments do not represent, described in a note to V42. In assembling, the same Latin term was checked for one rendering within each version and against the conventions: in Xylander's English species is now 'species' throughout (X15 and X26 had 'kinds'), Exodius is 'exodius' in both versions (Xylander's English had 'exodion'), two term entries were added (species; genus in its general sense), the term lists were merged (eleven entries are common to both versions), and unmarked or irregular reference marks were normalised to [checked] or [from memory]. The last full comparison with the Latin found no omission and no wrong number or ratio bracket; the Russian and English brackets agree in every segment. Boethius was read in Friedlein's edition (in a scan and in a transcription) and the dictionaries through online services; references to Theon, Cleonides, Gaudentius, Ptolemy and the Sectio canonis are mostly from memory and are marked so. A reviewer should check first V18, X32, V44–45 and X38, then the notes on paraphony and antiphony (X31, V36–37) against Theon of Smyrna, the from-memory references to the Greek theorists, and the description of the diagram at V42 against the page image. One item of possibly forbidden material was met, as a title in a search-result list only; it was not opened and not used.
